# Project Nexus

## Go-to-Market and Messaging Strategy

**Prepared for Westside Union**  
**Version 1.1 - July 23, 2026**

---

## Document purpose

This document explains how Westside Union should introduce, sell, demonstrate, price, and grow Project Nexus. It is written for the first market: local business owners who want practical help but do not want to become AI experts.

The strategy is to sell a defined business result through a managed service, then expand after trust and measurable value have been established.

---

## Current launch and design decision

The first public deliverable is the Project Nexus landing page. It will be adapted from the supplied `agentic-build-and-orchestrate-ai-agents-while-you-sleep/` Next.js template. Phase 1 must not include login, registration, or dashboard links.

Preserve the template’s warm off-white visual system, lightweight typography, bento-card composition, spacing, responsive behavior, and suitable UI components. Replace every occurrence of Agentic branding, developer-platform messaging, original pricing, unsupported proof, unsupported compliance claims, external destinations, metadata, and unapproved remote imagery/video.

The original template’s developer-focused “build agents” proposition must become an owner-focused Nexus story:

1. One assistant, many owner jobs
2. Industry-ready toolkits
3. Familiar channels
4. Managed setup
5. Proactive suggestions
6. Predictable pricing
7. Human approval and control
8. Consultation CTA

The future dashboard should share this Nexus-adapted design system while removing cinematic hero treatment, glitch/WebGL effects, and marketing-heavy animation.

## 1. Market position

Project Nexus sits between self-service AI software and expensive custom AI consulting.

| Alternative | Customer burden | Customization | Ongoing management |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generic chatbot or AI subscription | High | Low | Customer |
| Industry point solution | Medium | Narrow | Vendor |
| Automation freelancer | Medium | Project-specific | Often limited |
| Traditional custom AI consultancy | Low after project | High | Expensive |
| Project Nexus | Low | Focused and expandable | Westside Union |

Nexus should not compete on having the most models, agents, or technical features. It should compete on:

- Understanding the owner's actual workflow
- Fast and guided setup
- Familiar interaction channels
- Predictable monthly costs
- Human approval and control
- Ongoing monitoring and improvement
- A path from one useful assistant to a broader system

## 2. Core promise

Recommended master message:

> Your business assistant, built around the way you work and managed by Westside Union.

Supporting message:

> Nexus helps organize inquiries, follow-ups, information, and routine owner tasks through familiar tools, with predictable costs and human control.

Short form:

> One assistant. Your business knowledge. Managed for you.

The message should always begin with the business problem. Technical architecture belongs in later due-diligence conversations, not the homepage headline.

---

## 3. Initial ideal customer profile

The strongest first customer is:

- An owner-led business with one to five locations
- Receiving repeated customer or internal inquiries
- Losing owner time to coordination and follow-up
- Already using email, calendars, booking, messaging, or a CRM
- Able to identify one recurring workflow with visible value
- Willing to participate in a structured pilot
- Comfortable keeping a human in control of important actions

Avoid first-wave customers who:

- Expect a complete replacement for multiple employees
- Cannot define any recurring process
- Require regulated autonomous decisions
- Demand unlimited customization at a low fixed price
- Lack stable business information or process ownership
- Need complex legacy integration before a pilot can begin

---

## 4. Jobs the owner hires Nexus to do

Owners do not hire Nexus because they want an agent framework. They hire it because they want to:

- Stop answering the same questions
- Avoid losing inquiries
- Know what needs attention
- Follow up consistently
- Prepare routine communication faster
- Keep business information organized
- Reduce time spent switching between systems
- See the cost and outcome of AI in one place

The sales discovery should identify one of these jobs and convert it into a measurable pilot.

## 5. Product ladder

### Nexus Cloud

For an owner who wants a managed assistant without dedicated hardware.

**Current starting offer: setup from CAD $500 + CAD $299/month**

Position it as:

> The fastest way to give your business a tailored assistant with monitoring, updates, and an included monthly AI allowance.

### Nexus Managed

For an owner who needs more workflows, integrations, proactive notifications, expanded task automation, and monthly optimization.

**Current starting offer: setup from CAD $1,000 + CAD $499/month**

### Nexus Edge

For businesses that benefit from local processing, private knowledge services, or reduced cloud dependency.

**Current starting offer: CAD $599/month; setup quoted after discovery**

Position it as:

> A managed local AI appliance combined with secure cloud capabilities when they add value.

Do not call it a Mac mini rental. The product is a managed service with an appliance.

### Nexus Enterprise

For multiple departments, locations, advanced controls, private deployment, or custom integrations.

**Current offer: custom setup and monthly plan**

Position it as:

> The Nexus experience extended across teams, workflows, and enterprise systems.

## 6. Pricing presentation

The customer-facing price should have three understandable parts:

1. Deployment
2. Managed monthly plan
3. Clearly defined optional usage and add-ons

### Deployment includes

- Workflow discovery
- Assistant configuration
- Business knowledge setup
- Standard integrations
- Permission and approval rules
- Testing
- Owner onboarding
- Go-live support

### Monthly plan includes

- Hosting or appliance management
- Monitoring and backups
- Security and runtime updates
- Included AI allowance
- Support
- A defined optimization allowance
- Usage and outcome reporting

### Add-ons

- Extra location
- Extra assistant role
- Voice or phone
- Paid messaging channel
- Custom integration
- Custom workflow
- Additional optimization time
- Usage capacity pack

Never describe the monthly payment as maintenance alone. The recurring product is continued reliability, governance, reporting, and improvement.

---

## 7. Predictable cost message

Customer concern:

> What if the AI bill suddenly becomes expensive?

Recommended answer:

> Every Nexus plan includes a defined monthly allowance. You can see what has been used, receive alerts before the limit, and choose whether additional usage is allowed. There is no uncapped AI spending by default.

Explain local and cloud routing only if relevant:

> Nexus can use efficient local processing for routine work and approved cloud services for tasks that need more capability. The plan controls when additional cost is allowed.

Do not promise zero usage cost. Voice, messaging, search, and third-party services can be more expensive than text-model usage and must remain visible.

## 9. Barbershop and salon message

### Headline

> Spend more time with clients and less time chasing messages.

### Problems

- Questions arrive while the owner is serving a client
- Location, service, hiring, and collaboration inquiries get mixed together
- Cancellations and unanswered follow-ups create lost time

### Nexus outcome

- Organizes incoming questions
- Uses approved business information
- Routes customers to the existing booking process
- Prepares or sends approved follow-up
- Gives the owner a daily attention list

### Demo story

A customer asks about a service and the best location. Nexus answers from approved information, links the existing booking page, records the inquiry, and flags a special request for owner review.

### Important boundary

Nexus should complement the booking platform, not pretend to replace it unless a separate booking integration is specifically implemented.

---

## 10. Restaurant message

### Headline

> Keep guest questions and follow-ups moving, even during the rush.

### Problems

- Staff cannot answer every message during service
- Hours, menus, events, catering, and reservation questions repeat
- Special requests are easily lost across channels

### Nexus outcome

- Answers approved routine questions
- Organizes event and catering inquiries
- Routes reservation requests to the correct system
- Flags sensitive or unusual requests
- Gives the owner a summary after service

### Demo story

A guest asks about a private event. Nexus collects the date, group size, and contact details, explains that availability must be confirmed, and creates a structured follow-up for the manager.

### Important boundary

Nexus must not invent allergen, safety, pricing, availability, or reservation commitments.

## 16. Sales discovery

The first call should diagnose workflow pain rather than demonstrate random AI features.

### Discovery questions

- Which repeated task interrupts you most?
- Where do customer requests arrive?
- What happens when you are unavailable?
- Which follow-up is most often missed?
- Which information do you repeatedly search for?
- What should the assistant never do without asking?
- What would make the first 30 days clearly worthwhile?
- Which systems already hold the necessary information?
- Who owns the process?
- What monthly cost feels reasonable if the result is achieved?

### Qualification output

Produce a one-page pilot definition:

- Problem
- Current process
- Proposed Nexus workflow
- Included channel and integrations
- Human approvals
- Success measures
- Exclusions
- Deployment and monthly price

## 17. Demo design

The demo should last approximately 10 to 15 minutes and tell one coherent story.

### Demo sequence

1. Show a realistic inquiry in the customer's familiar channel.
2. Show Nexus using approved business knowledge.
3. Show an unusual or sensitive action requiring owner approval.
4. Show the owner approving or taking over.
5. Show the dashboard task, result, and remaining allowance.
6. Show a daily summary.

### What not to demo

- A long conversation about AI itself
- Model switching for its own sake
- Developer consoles
- Unreliable autonomous browsing
- A dozen disconnected features
- Sensitive production data

The prospect should leave remembering the outcome, not the technical machinery.

## 18. Pilot offer

### Recommended terms

- One clearly defined workflow
- One primary assistant
- One channel
- Up to two standard integrations
- Defined business knowledge
- Human approval for customer-facing actions during pilot
- Included usage allowance
- 90-day initial term
- 50% of deployment at signing
- 50% at go-live
- Success review at 30, 60, and 90 days

### Why a paid pilot

A paid pilot creates shared commitment, covers real setup work, and tests willingness to pay. A founding-client discount can be exchanged for structured feedback, reasonable case-study rights, and a fixed scope.

## 19. Success metrics

Choose no more than five primary measures for each pilot.

Possible metrics:

- Median response time
- Number of inquiries captured
- Percentage of eligible follow-ups completed
- Owner hours saved
- Number of owner takeovers
- Correction rate
- Appointment, quote, or lead outcomes influenced
- Cost per completed workflow
- Monthly allowance utilization
- Customer satisfaction

### Estimating time saved

Use a transparent formula:

```text
eligible tasks completed
x average manual minutes per task
- review and correction time
= estimated net time saved
```

### Estimating financial value

Use conservative assumptions:

```text
net hours saved x agreed hourly value
+ contribution from recovered outcomes
- Nexus variable costs
= estimated value
```

Do not present estimates as guaranteed savings.

## 20. Objection handling

### "Why not just use ChatGPT?"

> ChatGPT is a powerful general tool. Nexus is configured around your business information, channels, permissions, and workflow, and Westside Union manages it for you. The difference is similar to buying software versus having a managed business system.

### "Will it replace my staff?"

> The first Nexus package is designed to help the owner and team with repetitive work, not to make employment decisions. You choose what it can do, and important actions can require approval.

### "What if it says the wrong thing?"

> We start with a narrow scope, approved information, logs, and human approval for sensitive communication. We measure corrections and improve the workflow before adding autonomy.

### "Will the AI bill become unpredictable?"

> Your plan includes an allowance, dashboard tracking, and alerts. Additional spending is blocked or requires your approval unless you choose a capped automatic top-up.

### "Is my business information private?"

> Nexus uses organization-specific access controls and approved providers. We define what data is used, where it is processed, how long it is retained, and who can access it. Private and local options are available when justified.

### "Do I need a new app?"

> Normal interaction can happen through an approved familiar channel. In Phase 2, the web dashboard will provide approvals, history, settings, and usage, and will work on a phone without a separate installation.

### "What are OpenClaw, Hermes, and Pi?"

> They are possible components Westside Union may use behind Nexus. You do not need to choose or manage them. We select and maintain the appropriate tools for the agreed workflow.

## 21. Trust and risk messaging

Trust should be a central product feature.

Customer-facing trust commitments:

- You decide what Nexus may access
- You decide which actions need approval
- Activity is recorded
- Spending has a limit
- You can take over
- Westside Union monitors the service
- Your business data remains yours

Avoid absolute security or accuracy claims. Explain concrete controls and contract terms.

## 22. Customer journey

Industry page, local referral, partner introduction, workshop, or case study.

One problem, one workflow, explicit price and boundaries.

Business information, access authorization, policy choices, and owner training.

Human approval and close monitoring.

Deliver a visible result in the first week.

Review corrections, failures, usage, and outcome measures.

Add one workflow, role, location, channel, or Edge capability at a time.

### 9. Renewal and referral

Review value in business language and request a referral only after a proven outcome.

## 26. Expansion from local business to enterprise

The brand promise remains consistent:

> Nexus adapts to the business and hides technical complexity.

What changes:

| Local business | Enterprise |
|---|---|
| One owner assistant | Department and role assistants |
| One or two integrations | Enterprise systems |
| Simple approvals | Formal policy and governance |
| Standard dashboard | Audit, analytics, and administration |
| Shared cloud service | Dedicated or private deployment |
| Best-effort support terms | Defined service objectives |

Do not dilute the initial local-business message by placing every future enterprise capability on the first homepage. Use a separate enterprise page when the offer is ready.

## 27. 90-day launch plan

### Days 1-30 - Package

- Choose one primary vertical
- Define one owner workflow
- Finalize scope, exclusions, pricing, and terms
- Build the industry demo
- Create landing page and discovery form
- Define baseline and success measures

### Days 31-60 - Pilot

- Recruit two to three paid founding customers
- Complete onboarding and launch
- Review failures and corrections weekly
- Record support time and variable cost
- Improve the reusable blueprint

### Days 61-90 - Prove

- Complete outcome review
- Produce one approved case study
- Refine price and onboarding
- Decide whether the vertical is repeatable
- Add only the highest-value missing feature
- Begin a focused referral and partner campaign

## 29. Messaging principles

1. Lead with an owner problem.
2. Show one result before describing the platform.
3. Say assistant before agent.
4. Say allowance before tokens.
5. Say approval before autonomy.
6. Say managed service before model names.
7. Use industry examples.
8. Make boundaries visible.
9. Avoid replacement claims.
10. Prove value with conservative measurements.
11. Keep the first sale narrow.
12. Expand after trust.

## Conclusion

Project Nexus should enter the market as a practical managed assistant for local business owners, not as a universal AI operating system. The platform vision can remain ambitious while the first sales promise stays precise.

The winning message is:

> Nexus learns the approved information and routine behind your business, helps through familiar channels, asks before important actions, and keeps the monthly cost visible. Westside Union sets it up and manages it for you.
